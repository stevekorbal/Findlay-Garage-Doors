## Understanding Your Garage Door Torsion Spring System

Your overhead garage door is likely the heaviest moving part of your home, often weighing between **150 to 350 pounds**. While many homeowners assume the electric opener does the heavy lifting, it is actually the high-tension **torsion springs** mounted above the door opening that perform 90% of the mechanical work.

In Northwest Ohio, cold winters and heavy daily usage exert tremendous stress on metal springs. Over time, metal fatigue weakens the coiled steel until it inevitably fails. Knowing the early warning signs of spring degradation can prevent you from getting trapped inside your garage during a morning commute.

Here are the **5 most common warning signs** that your garage door springs require immediate professional attention in Findlay, OH.

---

## 1. Loud Bang Sounding Like a Gunshot Inside the Garage

When a high-tension torsion spring breaks under load, it releases stored kinetic energy instantaneously. This creates a deafening bang that many Hancock County homeowners mistake for a gunshot or a falling object in the attic.

If you hear a sudden loud noise in your garage, inspect the metal bar directly above your door headers. A broken torsion spring will display a clear 2-inch to 3-inch gap between the coils.

> **Safety Warning:** Never attempt to operate an opener or manually lift a garage door with a snapped torsion spring. Doing so can burn out your opener motor or bend the top panel tracks.

---

## 2. The Door Opens Only a Few Inches and Stops

Modern automatic garage door openers are engineered with built-in safety sensors and force limiters. When a torsion spring breaks, the weight of the door increases dramatically.

When you press your wall console or remote control:
- The opener motor may groan under load.
- The door will lift 3 to 6 inches off the concrete floor.
- The opener safety system will trigger, immediately stopping or reversing the door.

This is a classic symptom that the opener motor is resisting lifted weight without spring counterbalance assist.

---

## 3. Visible Gaps or Stretched Coils in the Spring System

Take a visual look at the torsion spring mounted horizontally above the closed garage door:

1. **Gaps in the Coil:** A clean break creates an obvious separation between the tightly wound coils.
2. **Rust & Elongation:** Heavy rust accumulation binds spring coils together, causing uneven tension and metal decay during damp Ohio spring thaws.
3. **Loose Cables:** When tension drops suddenly, the lift cables on the sides of the door lose tension and spool loosely off the cable drums.

---

## 4. The Garage Door Rises Jerkily or Crookedly

If your garage door utilizes double torsion springs (common on standard two-car garages) and one spring snaps while the other remains under partial tension, the door will rise unevenly:

- One side of the door hangs lower than the other.
- The top rollers bind inside the vertical tracks.
- The cables may slip off the drum on the un-tensioned side.

Attempting to force a crooked door open will dislodge rollers and potentially drop the door completely off its tracks.

---

## 5. The Emergency Release Cord Is Impossible to Pull Manually

If you pull the red emergency manual release cord and attempt to lift the door by hand, a properly balanced door should lift easily with one hand. If the door feels like an immovable block of lead, the spring counterbalance system is completely non-functional.
